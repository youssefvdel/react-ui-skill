# HalftoneReveal
**Registry**: `HalftoneReveal-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: HalftoneReveal/HalftoneReveal.tsx
**What it does** — Renders a photograph as a screen of halftone dots — mono, duotone or colour — at a chosen angle and cell size. The cursor paints a soft brush mask that swells the dots back into the real photo along its path; the reveal lingers, then the print closes over it again.
**Props** — `src` (string, Unsplash default) — the image; `mode` (`mono` | `duotone` | `color`, mono); `shape` (`dot` | `ellipse` | `square` | `diamond` | `line` | `cross`, dot); `cellSize` (number, 6) — dot pitch; `dotScale` (number, 1) — dot fatness; `inkColor` / `paperColor` (string, #120f17 / #ffffff); `revealRadius` (number, 160) and `linger` (number, 1.2) — brush size and how long the photo stays open; `wander` (boolean, false) — self-drifting brush when the pointer is away.
**Motion** — WebGL2 two-pass (half-resolution mask, then the view). The pointer is the trigger: move to paint, click for a radial burst, 1.4 s intro where the print resolves from nothing. Reduced motion removes the intro and the wander, but the brush and click burst still animate — gate those yourself.
**Cost** — No deps, ~9 KB gzip, one texture, DPR ≤ 2, rAF only while visible and awake. The image is fetched with `crossOrigin="anonymous"`, so it must be same-origin or CORS-enabled — ARKRETE's own photos are fine, a CDN without headers is not. Prerenders as a black canvas.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — About, the firm section: one archival frame — workshop, drawing, material sample — printed in bone ink on near-black, resolving under the pointer. One image only; the projects stack must stay clean photography.
**Verdict** — adapt — a genuinely editorial print treatment that matches the atelier register, at the price of a single WebGL island.
