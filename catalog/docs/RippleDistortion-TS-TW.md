# RippleDistortion
**Registry**: `RippleDistortion-TS-TW` · **Deps**: ogl@^1.0.11 · **Files**: RippleDistortion/RippleDistortion.tsx
**What it does** — Paints a photograph into a WebGL canvas and drags its pixels sideways under the cursor, leaving a wake of decaying concentric ripples that refract the image. Optional RGB split, specular glint, tint and grayscale sit on top of the displacement.
**Props** — `src` (string, stock photo) — the image being warped; `strength` (0.2) — how far pixels are pushed; `brushSize` (150) — footprint of each ripple; `swirl` (1) — rotates the push around its centre; `fade` (3) — how long a wake lingers; `dispersion` (0) — RGB split at the wave front; `glint` (0) — specular highlight on crests; `quality` ('low') — resolution of the displacement buffer.
**Motion** — Pointer move spawns an instanced wave every 15 px, each growing then decaying on its own; a click can add a stronger one. Waves live in a 100-slot ring buffer and are drawn additively into a render target that then warps the photo. WebGL via ogl; the rAF loop runs from mount to unmount with no intersection or tab-visibility gate.
**Cost** — ogl is roughly 20 KB gzip, plus a full-resolution image texture and DPR up to 2. Every frame costs two passes whether or not anything moved, and reduced motion only stops new waves, not the render loop. Client-only: prerender emits an empty black div, the `<img>` and its alt text are replaced by a canvas, and `src` is fetched with `crossOrigin="anonymous"`, so it needs CORS.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — no fit. Photography is the argument on every page; a cursor wake that distorts it is decoration aimed at the one thing that must stay legible, and the tint defaults to violet.
**Verdict** — skip — 20 KB of WebGL and a never-idle GPU loop to bend the photography, which is the site's argument, not its ornament.
