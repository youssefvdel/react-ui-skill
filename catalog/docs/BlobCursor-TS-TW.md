# BlobCursor
**Registry**: `BlobCursor-TS-TW` · **Deps**: three@^0.180.0 · **Files**: BlobCursor/BlobCursor.tsx
**What it does** — Replaces the pointer with a liquid-metal blob that lags behind the cursor, stretches into a tapering tail, wobbles at rest, drips droplets when moved fast and bursts into spheres on click. Comes in chrome, jelly, pearl or ink finishes.
**Props** — `material` ('chrome' | 'jelly' | 'pearl' | 'ink', 'chrome') — surface finish; `color` (string, `#3B82F6`) — blob tint; `size` (number, 140) — diameter in px; `trail` (number, 16) — segments of tail, 0 for a bare blob; `lag` (number, 0.55) — how far it drags behind the pointer; `goo` (number, 1) — how strongly the segments merge; `splash` (boolean, true) — droplets on press; `hideCursor` (boolean, true) — hides the native cursor on the parent element.
**Motion** — Pointer-driven and continuous: a hand-written spring integrator runs a head plus up to sixteen trail bodies, up to 96 instanced droplets and a separate splash body, all drawn through three.js as a raymarched 2D SDF on one full-screen plane with a custom depth material and 2048px VSM shadows. The loop stops after 0.4 seconds of calm. Reduced motion stiffens the springs, stops the idle morph, drip and click burst — but the blob still follows the pointer.
**Cost** — three.js alone is roughly 160 KB gzipped before the `RoomEnvironment` import, against a 150 KB initial budget; the component adds 9 KB of source, an 8-step raymarch over 48 primitive parts per pixel, a PMREM environment and a shadow map. Client-only, fixed overlay, and it removes the cursor from its parent — fatal on a form. `hideCursor=false` is the only sane setting here.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — No fit — a chrome cursor is a portfolio toy, not a fit-out contractor's first impression.
**Verdict** — skip — three.js blows the entire JS budget and hiding the cursor in an inquiry form is hostile.
