# SplitText
**Registry**: `SplitText-TS-TW` · **Deps**: gsap@^3.13.0, @gsap/react@^2.1.2 · **Files**: SplitText/SplitText.tsx
**What it does** — Splits a line of text into characters, words or lines and tweens them in one after another, with a choice of rise, fade, blur, flip, pop, skew or wave. It can also replay on hover, click or a timer, or bow each letter away from the pointer.
**Props** — `text` (string, required) — the copy to split; `splitType` ('chars' | 'words' | 'lines' | 'words, chars', 'chars') — unit that animates; `effect` ('rise' | 'fade' | 'blur' | 'flip' | 'pop' | 'skew' | 'wave', 'rise') — the entrance look; `trigger` ('view' | 'load' | 'hover' | 'click', 'view') — what starts it; `delay` (number, 50) — milliseconds between pieces; `duration` (number, 1.25) — seconds per piece; `mask` (boolean, false) — clips each piece behind a line mask so it wipes up; `hoverEffect` ('none' | 'lift' | 'wave', 'none') — per-letter pointer response after the entrance.
**Motion** — A GSAP timeline over the SplitText output; ScrollTrigger fires it at `threshold` / `rootMargin` for `trigger="view"`. The hover lift runs its own spring loop writing `translate` per character. Under reduced motion it renders the text untouched and marks the animation complete.
**Cost** — GSAP core plus ScrollTrigger, SplitText and @gsap/react: roughly 40–50 KB gzip, all client-side, and one DOM node per character. The tag ships `visibility: hidden` until fonts load and the split runs, so a failed bundle leaves the headline blank — bad for a statically prerendered hero. Reverts and rebuilds the split on every text or prop change.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — Home, the monument wordmark at first paint: one slow per-letter rise on the firm name, then nothing else on the page moves until the reel.
**Verdict** — adapt — the letter rise belongs on the hero, but 50 KB of GSAP for one entrance is a third of the budget; rebuild it as CSS spans with staggered transitions.
