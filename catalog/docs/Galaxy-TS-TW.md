# Galaxy
**Registry**: `Galaxy-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: Galaxy/Galaxy.tsx
**What it does** — A WebGL2 starfield of two to eight parallax layers: hash-scattered stars drift toward the viewer, twinkle, flare, and shove away from the pointer. An optional 2.2-second warp reveals it, and a click kicks the field into a short jump; colours come from the star hash or a supplied palette.
**Props** — `focal` ([number, number], [0.5, 0.5]) — vanishing point in normalised canvas space; `layers` (number, 4) — depth layers, clamped 2–8; `density` (number, 1) — stars per layer; `starSpeed` (number, 0.5) — forward flight rate; `starColors` (string[], none) — up to four fixed star colours, replacing the procedural hue; `mouseRepulsion` (boolean, true) — stars part around the pointer; `clickWarp` (boolean, true) — click triggers the stretch jump; `transparent` (boolean, true) — clears to alpha instead of painting a background.
**Motion** — One continuous rAF loop, all of it in the fragment shader: drift, twinkle, slow rotation, pointer repulsion, warp, intro fade. Reduced motion sets the time multiplier to zero and renders a single still frame. Needs WebGL2; without it the component renders an empty box, silently.
**Cost** — 22.8 KB of source, no packages. The fragment shader walks 9 cells × up to 8 layers for every pixel at min(DPR, 2), and compiles up to four `#define` variants (palette / repel / warp) on first use. Pauses when offscreen or the tab is hidden. The canvas has no poster, so prerender ships an empty transparent block.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — No fit. The home hero is a monument and a reel of rooms; a literal galaxy is a different world, and a full-screen shader would fight both the wordmark and the photography.
**Verdict** — skip — expensive full-screen GPU work for a space backdrop the brand never asked for.
