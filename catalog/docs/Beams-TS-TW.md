# Beams
**Registry**: `Beams-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: Beams/Beams.tsx
**What it does** — Fills its container with glossy ribbons that run at an angle across a near-black ground, each lane lit like brushed metal, and the highlight slides along the beams toward the pointer. An intro sweeps the light source in from below over 1.6 seconds.
**Props** — `color` (string, `#ffffff`) — tint of the reflected light; `beamWidth` (number, 140) — px per ribbon lane; `rotation` (number, 30) — beam angle in degrees; `speed` (number, 1) — drift rate, 0 freezes the field; `waveLength` (number, 233) — scale of the ripple along each beam; `depth` (number, 1) — surface relief and gloss; `mouseInteraction` (boolean, true) — whether the pointer pulls the light; `quality` (number, 0.75) — internal render scale from 0.25 to 1.
**Motion** — Continuous shader time, no animation library: two WebGL2 fragment programs, the first writing a lit height field and the second tonemapping, blooming from mip levels and adding grain and a feathered edge mask. The pointer adds a gaussian light lift, lerped at 7/s; the loop parks itself when the component is offscreen, the tab is hidden or `paused` is set. Reduced motion freezes the clock, skips the intro and ignores the pointer.
**Cost** — No dependencies, about 7 KB gzipped source. Two programs, a full-screen triangle and a float field texture (RGBA8 fallback) capped at 1.6 MP whose mipmaps are regenerated every frame — a real GPU load on integrated graphics and phones, and quality 0.75 is already a compromise. The canvas is inert in markup, so prerender is safe, but there is no static fallback beyond the background colour.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — Home, behind the monument wordmark — the only full-bleed slot where an ambient field could sit.
**Verdict** — skip — the room reel is the argument; a ribbon field is a second visual language, and in brass it would spend the accent on atmosphere rather than action.
