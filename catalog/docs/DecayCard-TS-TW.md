# DecayCard
**Registry**: `DecayCard-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: `DecayCard/DecayCard.tsx`
**What it does** — A photo card that tears and melts where the pointer moves quickly, then knits itself back together when the hand slows. At rest it drifts and tilts slightly, as if the image were printed on something soft.
**Props** — `image` (string, unsplash default) — the photo · `width` / `height` (number, `300` / `400`) — card box · `intensity` (number 0–2, `0.5`) — how far pixels are displaced · `sensitivity` (number 0–1, `0.5`) — pointer speed needed to trigger it · `idle` (number 0–1, `0.35`) — resting drift · `grain` (number 0–1, `0.25`) — noise frequency · `detail` (number 1–8, `5`) — noise octaves · `scope` (`'window' | 'card'`, `'window'`) — whether the whole page or only the card drives the decay.
**Motion** — Pointer velocity is turned into a simplex-turbulence UV displacement inside a fragment shader; the tear eases back at `recovery` once the pointer slows. The card itself translates and rotates toward the cursor within `travel` and `tilt`, and an idle noise term keeps the surface alive. No trigger other than pointer movement; there is no click or scroll behaviour.
**Cost** — Raw WebGL2, no dependencies, 17.2 KB source (5.7 KB gzip). One window-level `pointermove` listener per card, plus a ResizeObserver and an IntersectionObserver; the rAF loop halves its draw rate once settled. The shader samples a hard-coded 600×750 stage, so any photo that is not 4:5 is cropped. Falls back to a plain `<img>` when WebGL2 is missing.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — Projects rooms would be the only home, and there the effect is backwards: it damages the room photography that is the entire argument.
**Verdict** — skip — a melt gimmick with a fixed 4:5 stage, applied to the one asset that must stay intact.
