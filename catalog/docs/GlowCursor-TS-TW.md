# GlowCursor
**Registry**: `GlowCursor-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: GlowCursor/GlowCursor.tsx
**What it does** — A soft light pours from the cursor and drifts behind it as smoke, cooling and thinning as it fades; clicking throws a small burst of jets. It can fill a section as a background layer with children stacked above it, and the light can chase the pointer with spring lag or snap to it.
**Props** — `color` (string, #5f8bff) — light colour; `intensity` (number, 1.3) — emission strength; `trailWidth` (number, 3) — core width in px; `linger` (number, 1.4) — seconds the dye survives; `glow` (number, 1.2) — bloom gain; `followSpeed` (number, 0.6) — lag between cursor and emitter; `quality` ('low' | 'medium' | 'high', 'high') — simulation resolution; `clickBurst` (boolean, true) — radial puff on press.
**Motion** — A full Navier–Stokes fluid simulation on the GPU: eleven programs covering splat, advection, MacCormack correction, divergence, Jacobi pressure, gradient subtraction, bloom down/up and display, all driven by pointer movement. Reduced motion removes the velocity and pressure passes and only decays a static dye. Requires WebGL2 with `EXT_color_buffer_float`.
**Cost** — 34.9 KB, no packages, but several float framebuffer pairs at min(DPR, 2) and up to sixty full-screen passes a second while the pointer is active — the heaviest thing in this batch. Client-only; the canvas needs a sized parent, and unsupported devices get nothing rather than a fallback.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — No fit. A blue fluid light drifting over near-black is a screensaver; even retuned to brass it would be decoration laid on top of the photography, which is supposed to be the argument.
**Verdict** — skip — a fluid solver for a cursor ornament is the clearest case of cost without a job in this batch.
