# FallingText
**Registry**: `FallingText-TS-TW` · **Deps**: matter-js@^0.20.0 · **Files**: FallingText/FallingText.tsx
**What it does** — Splits a headline into words or letters, then drops them as rigid bodies that tumble, collide and settle into a pile at the bottom of the block. The pieces can be dragged and thrown, and after a quiet delay they fly back into the line.
**Props** — `trigger` ('auto' | 'hover' | 'click' | 'scroll', 'auto') — what releases the pieces; `split` ('words' | 'letters', 'words') — unit that falls; `variant` ('text' | 'tiles', 'text') — plain type or extruded tiles; `gravity` (number, 1) — pull; `bounciness` (number, 0.2) — restitution; `scatter` (number, 0.5) — sideways kick at release; `draggable` (boolean, true) — grab and throw with the pointer; `restoreDelay` (number, 0) — seconds of stillness before the text reassembles.
**Motion** — A Matter.js engine stepped on rAF with a fixed timestep; each piece is an absolutely positioned span whose transform is written from its body. Pointer velocity pushes nearby bodies, a drag constraint picks one up, and the engine runs until every piece sleeps (or is paused offscreen by an IntersectionObserver). Reduced motion never drops the text at all.
**Cost** — matter-js is about 28 KB gzip on top of 23 KB of source, and one body plus one positioned span per word or letter — a twelve-word headline is a dozen bodies, letters sixty. The engine is DOM-transform bound, not canvas, so it competes with scrolling while the pile settles. The text is in the prerendered HTML but only readable once JS has measured and re-laid it out.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — no fit — the home wordmark is a monument, and a headline that collapses into a heap is a joke at its expense.
**Verdict** — skip — 28 KB of physics and a comic interaction for a firm selling restraint.
