# Ferrofluid
**Registry**: `Ferrofluid-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: Ferrofluid/Ferrofluid.tsx
**What it does** — Renders a black sea of magnetic fluid in perspective: slow swells roll across it, a field of spikes rises and travels, and the pointer pulls up a crown of spikes that collapses when it leaves. It is lit like a studio material: gloss, rim light, and the reflection of two softbox panels sliding across the swell.
**Props** — `colors` (string[], three whites) — palette for the fluid and its highlights; `speed` (number, 0.5) — how fast the field travels; `spikeHeight` (number, 1) — height of the spikes; `sharpness` (number, 2.5) — how narrow the tips are; `magnetism` (number, 1) — strength of the cursor's pull; `mouseInteraction` (boolean, true) — cursor crown on or off; `clickPulse` (boolean, true) — ripple on click; `paused` (boolean, false) — freeze the simulation.
**Motion** — Custom WebGL: a grid fluid simulation ping-ponging between two textures, then a per-pixel bounded heightfield march of up to 64 steps to shade the surface; the cursor casts a ray to place its crown. The rAF stops offscreen or hidden. Reduced motion freezes the flow and skips the intro swell.
**Cost** — 33 KB of source (about 9 KB gzip) and no dependencies, but it is the heaviest pixel cost in this batch: 64 march steps per pixel at DPR up to 2, plus two simulation passes and a bounds pass every frame. Expect it to miss 60 fps on integrated graphics at full width, and it has no static still — the canvas is blank without JS.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — no fit — the only full-bleed surfaces are the hero and the reel, and both are photography of rooms, not liquid metal.
**Verdict** — skip — the most beautiful thing in the batch and the least employable: a 64-step march per pixel for decoration.
