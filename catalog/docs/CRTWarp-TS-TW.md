# CRTWarp
**Registry**: `CRTWarp-TS-TW` · **Deps**: none · **Files**: CRTWarp/CRTWarp.tsx
**What it does** — Draws a blocky plasma on a curved CRT: a coarse cell grid with phosphor afterglow, RGB beam scanlines, an aperture mask, bloom, grain, flicker, interference and vignette, plus a power-on intro. Clicking the screen flips to another plasma channel with a rolling, tearing transition.
**Props** — `color` (string, `#c755f7`) — phosphor colour; `backgroundColor` (string, `#120f17`) — ground and card fill; `columns` (number, 80) — cells across the grid; `levels` (number, 3) — quantisation steps per cell; `persistence` (number, 0.4) — afterglow retained between frames; `curvature` (number, 0.25) — barrel distortion, 0 for flat; `scanlineStrength` (number, 0.6) — beam separation; `mouseReact` (boolean, true) — pointer magnet bends the beams.
**Motion** — Self-running, no library: four WebGL2 programs (cell field, glow, separable two-pass blur, display) ping-ponging between two render targets, with a 1.6s power-on intro, a 0.8s channel flip on click and a pointer magnet driven by a damped spring. Time is multiplied by `speed` (0.5 default); `fps` caps the loop, an IntersectionObserver pauses offscreen drawing and it stops rendering after about ninety quiet frames. Reduced motion freezes time, flicker, intro and magnet.
**Cost** — No dependencies and 8.8 KB gzipped source, but the heaviest GPU load here: a full-screen display shader at up to 2× DPR, an 80-column cell field (about 25 rows wide, 45 square) refreshed every frame and a quarter-resolution blur pair. Client-only — the canvas is created inside the effect, so prerender is an empty div with no text and nothing to index.
**Fit for ARKRETE** — No fit — a violet CRT is a different brand universe and would sit against every room photograph like a joke.
**Verdict** — skip — a very good demo of the wrong world, spending the frame budget on atmosphere.
